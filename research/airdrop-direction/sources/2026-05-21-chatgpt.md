**Thesis**

As of May 21, 2026, the best primary bet is a **sequenced dual-wedge platform**: sell **reputation anti-farmer** first, but build it on the same **AI judge/account-rollup/crawler** core that later supports creative and contribution scoring. I would **not** make “marketing spend judge” the company bet on its own; that market is bigger, but it is more crowded, less crypto-native, and requires ad-platform integrations the current product story does not obviously make cheap.

## 1. Market Pull

1. **Anti-farmer API/control plane for protocols and launchpads**  
This has the clearest pain today because the buyer can tie spend directly to avoided leakage: fake users dilute airdrops, grants, allowlists, and quest budgets. Budgets are lumpy around TGE, governance, grants, and campaign windows, but they survive bear markets better than “brand” tooling because the ROI is framed as loss prevention.

2. **White-label judge for Galxe/quest/launchpad ecosystems**  
This is a distribution-led version of anti-farmer: instead of convincing every protocol separately, you sell to the platforms that already intermediate campaigns. Deal sizes can be larger and stickier, but budget cycles are slower because platform partners will treat this as roadmap/vendor infrastructure rather than campaign spend.

3. **Contributor reputation network: protocol console + portable user score**  
This is the strongest long-term market if you can own both the protocol-side spend decision and the user-side reputation layer. The problem is timing: buyers understand anti-Sybil now, but “portable contribution reputation” is still a category-creation sell unless attached to an immediate workflow like airdrop eligibility or grant triage.

4. **Creative-spend judge sold to agencies and growth teams**  
This gets you into monthly SaaS budgets instead of token-event budgets, and agencies can become early resellers if the product helps them justify spend. The risk is that creative scoring buyers already have many web2-native tools, so you need a very specific crypto/community angle or you end up competing in a crowded adtech lane.

5. **Open-source rubric + hosted eval/data coop hybrid**  
Open-sourcing the rubric can create trust and distribution fast, especially if projects want transparent judging criteria instead of a black-box score. Revenue is indirect, though: you monetize hosted scoring, premium signals, private models, or a shared reputation dataset, which makes this less attractive if you need near-term cash but more attractive if the real goal is category ownership or an eventual acquihire/data-asset outcome.

## 2. Competitive Moat

1. **Anti-farmer API/control plane for protocols and launchpads**  
This lane is crowded at the identity layer: Human Passport/Gitcoin Passport, Trusta, and Galxe all already sell anti-Sybil or reputation primitives. The whitespace is not “another score,” but a **decisioning product** that combines crawled context, account rollups, rubric outputs, and explainable flags for specific distribution workflows.

2. **White-label judge for Galxe/quest/launchpad ecosystems**  
This is less crowded because most identity vendors want to own the end-user brand, while platforms want embedded infrastructure they can brand themselves. The moat comes from being the easiest drop-in service with clear APIs, fast scoring, and auditability, not from owning the final user-facing identity wallet.

3. **Contributor reputation network: protocol console + portable user score**  
The network play is the biggest moat if it works, because every judged campaign improves future ranking and fraud detection. The problem is bootstrap: incumbents already have passport/identity graphs, so your angle has to be “quality of contribution and intent,” not just uniqueness of wallet.

4. **Creative-spend judge sold to agencies and growth teams**  
This lane is crowded by adtech AI players such as Segwise and broader creative analytics vendors, and those firms already speak the buyer’s language. The whitespace is a narrow one: **crypto-native campaign quality scoring** that scores creators, communities, quest completions, and onchain downstream behavior, not just CTR/CPI.

5. **Open-source rubric + hosted eval/data coop hybrid**  
This creates a different moat: standard-setting rather than feature breadth. If your rubric becomes the default benchmark for “worthy participant” or “quality campaign,” the hosted layer compounds, but that moat is only real if the open source artifact is adopted by platforms, agencies, or foundations rather than admired by developers.

## 3. Technical Reuse

1. **Anti-farmer API/control plane for protocols and launchpads**  
This likely preserves the highest fraction of the current monorepo, roughly **70-85%**, because farming flags, account rollups, crawlers, and judge outputs all map directly. MVP is fast: keep the scoring/flags/rollup pipeline, add policy templates and reviewer dashboards, and delete or defer the arcade/demo surface if it distracts.

2. **White-label judge for Galxe/quest/launchpad ecosystems**  
Reuse is also high, around **65-80%**, because the same core engine can sit behind partner APIs. The main work is packaging: auth, multi-tenant scoring endpoints, embeddable explanations, rate limits, partner analytics, and less of the direct end-user app.

3. **Contributor reputation network: protocol console + portable user score**  
Reuse is medium-high, around **55-75%**, because the existing data and judge layers help, but you now need persistent identity resolution, user profiles, appeal flows, and score portability. Time-to-MVP is longer because the product stops being a tool and starts being a system of record.

4. **Creative-spend judge sold to agencies and growth teams**  
Reuse is medium at best, around **35-55%**, because rubric scoring survives but farming flags and wallet/account-rollup logic become much less central unless the creative product still scores crypto-native community actions. You would probably delete or freeze anti-Sybil-specific UX and instead build ad ingestion, creative asset evaluation, and reporting integrations.

5. **Open-source rubric + hosted eval/data coop hybrid**  
Reuse is medium-high, around **50-70%**, because the judge, crawlers, and rollups can become the hosted backbone while the open-source layer exposes schemas, rubrics, and maybe a local scorer. What gets deleted is most bespoke product UI; what gets added is docs, SDKs, reproducibility, and governance around public criteria.

## 4. GTM Motion

1. **Anti-farmer API/control plane for protocols and launchpads**  
This is classic founder-led enterprise sales, but the target list is finite and knowable: foundations, launchpads, grant programs, and quest operators. That actually suits a solo or small team if you stay narrow, because five to ten lighthouse logos matter more than thousands of signups.

2. **White-label judge for Galxe/quest/launchpad ecosystems**  
This is platform BD rather than pure enterprise field sales. It matches a small team if you can land one or two leverage accounts, but it is risky because each deal can stall for months on partner prioritization.

3. **Contributor reputation network: protocol console + portable user score**  
This requires mixed GTM: enterprise on one side and user growth on the other. That is the hardest fit for a small team because you are simultaneously selling infrastructure and seeding a network.

4. **Creative-spend judge sold to agencies and growth teams**  
This is the best PLG-adjacent motion of the five because marketers will sign up for dashboards and run experiments without procurement if the onboarding is instant. The problem is support burden and churn: small teams can drown in onboarding, integration requests, and proof-of-ROI debates unless the product is extremely self-serve.

5. **Open-source rubric + hosted eval/data coop hybrid**  
This is community-led GTM with optional enterprise upsell. It fits a small team if the founders are willing to trade speed of revenue for speed of trust and inbound, but it is a poor fit if the business needs fast, predictable ARR inside twelve months.

## 5. Brand Fit

1. **Anti-farmer API/control plane for protocols and launchpads**  
`airdrop.works` helps here because the brand is natively tied to distribution, eligibility, and fairness. It sounds like the place you would go to decide who should or should not receive an airdrop.

2. **White-label judge for Galxe/quest/launchpad ecosystems**  
The brand is acceptable but slightly narrow. It helps for crypto questing and distribution partners, but it may limit expansion into broader contributor reputation unless the sub-branding emphasizes the judging engine rather than the airdrop destination.

3. **Contributor reputation network: protocol console + portable user score**  
The brand partly helps and partly hurts. It helps with initial wedge credibility, but a portable reputation graph is larger than “airdrop,” so the company would eventually need a product architecture where `airdrop.works` is one application on top of a broader reputation layer.

4. **Creative-spend judge sold to agencies and growth teams**  
The brand hurts the most here. Agencies buying marketing tooling do not naturally want a product that sounds crypto-airdrop-specific unless your wedge is explicitly “web3 campaign quality,” in which case the niche framing is acceptable.

5. **Open-source rubric + hosted eval/data coop hybrid**  
Brand fit depends on what you open-source. If the rubric is framed around airdrop eligibility, the brand helps; if the goal is a general public standard for contribution quality, the brand becomes a limiting wrapper around a broader idea.

## 6. 90-Day Shape

1. **Anti-farmer API/control plane for protocols and launchpads**  
Wedge: ship a reviewer console that scores wallets/accounts/campaign participants with explainable farmer flags and policy presets for allowlists, grants, and airdrops. Kill it if, by day 90, you do not have at least **3 live design partners**, **1 paid pilot**, and evidence that teams change decisions based on your flags.

2. **White-label judge for Galxe/quest/launchpad ecosystems**  
Wedge: package the same core as an API-first partner product with scoring webhooks, exports, and co-branded explanations. Kill it if no platform partner will run a sandbox within 60 days, because partner-led motions die from calendar drift more than product gaps.

3. **Contributor reputation network: protocol console + portable user score**  
Platform: define the durable core as a **reputation graph** built from crawlers, account rollups, judge outputs, and policy outcomes. Success is not raw users; it is **repeat use across campaigns**, **appeal/resolution loops**, and at least **two independent protocols** reusing the same reputation primitives instead of one-off lists.

4. **Creative-spend judge sold to agencies and growth teams**  
Second wedge: offer a narrow “judge your creator/community campaign before you pay for distribution” product to a handful of crypto agencies, not the whole ad market. Kill it if agencies like the output but will only buy it as custom service work; that means it is a feature or consulting offer, not a product wedge.

5. **Open-source rubric + hosted eval/data coop hybrid**  
Trust layer: publish a lightweight open rubric/spec so buyers can see how quality and farmer-risk are judged, while keeping premium signals and hosted scoring private. Success is **adoption by others**: forks, SDK usage, inbound API keys, and at least one external team building against your schema; kill it if it generates attention but no usage or deal acceleration.

**Recommendation**

Primary bet for the next 12 months: **dual-wedge platform, sequenced as anti-farmer first and judge second**. Concretely, build the platform around a reusable reputation graph, sell **anti-farmer decisioning** to protocols and platforms now, and use the same engine to test a narrow **creative/contribution judge** with agencies and community operators.

If forced to choose a single wedge, choose **A: reputation anti-farmer** over **B: marketing spend judge**. The codebase you described already sounds optimized for **who deserves distribution** more than for **which ad creative deserves spend**, and that is where the existing assets appear to make productization cheapest.
