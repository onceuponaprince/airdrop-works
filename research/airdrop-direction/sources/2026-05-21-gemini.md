## 1. Sybil resistance mechanism design

### Problem framing

Sybil resistance is a **cost asymmetry** problem: an attacker must pay more to create fake identities than they gain from exploiting the system. Classical formulations (Douceur 2002; Levine et al. on trust in P2P) show that without a trusted authority or expensive resource, Sybil attacks are inevitable at scale. Web3 airdrops invert incentives: the attacker’s marginal cost per wallet approaches zero while upside is unbounded token value.

### Mechanism families

**Proof-of-personhood (PoP).** Proof of Humanity, BrightID, World ID, and Gitcoin Passport aggregate signals that a human controls an account. Strength: raises account-creation cost. Weakness: **one human ≠ one honest participant** — farmers pass PoP and still produce low-effort content at scale. PoP solves duplicate humans, not duplicate effort or quality gaming.

**Graph / behavioural Sybil detection.** SybilRank (Cao et al.), Ostra, and commercial successors (Cred, Trusta, ChainAware) use transaction graphs, funding paths, and temporal co-movement. Attack surface: sophisticated farmers use aged wallets, mixer routing, and “organic-looking” activity. Graph scores commoditize toward **cents-per-lookup API pricing**; moat shifts to proprietary labels and campaign integration.

**Stake, slash, and skin-in-the-game.** Token staking for eligibility (EigenLayer-adjacent thinking, retroactive airdrop “commitment” narratives) raises capital cost. Slashing requires observable misbehaviour — hard when farming is grey-area content, not on-chain fraud. Stake filters capital, not intent.

**Behavioural + content signals.** Second-layer scoring after identity — engagement velocity caps, originality checks, cross-account text similarity, LLM rubrics — addresses **quality Sybils**: many verified humans producing template content. Zama Creator Program (2025) and similar creator-incentive programs explicitly pair anti-bot gates with **monthly quality scoring**, signalling industry movement beyond stamp collection.

**Composable credential stacks.** Passport’s stamp model is **additive trust**: protocols set thresholds over heterogeneous attestations. Game theory: attackers optimize minimum-cost stamp bundle; defenders must raise marginal stamp cost faster than token upside. Whitespace for airdrop-works **Fork A**: not “another Passport stamp” but **post-identity contribution judge** — farming flags, rubric dimensions, account rollups layered on top of existing PoP adoption.

### Design principles (mechanism design lens)

1. **Separate identity from merit.** Identity mechanisms allocate *eligibility*; merit mechanisms allocate *weight*. Collapsing both into one score invites both Sybil and quality farming.
2. **Make gaming observable.** Rubrics with explainable dimensions (teaching value, originality, impact) create audit trails protocols need for governance legitimacy.
3. **Prefer continuous scoring over binary allowlists.** Binary Sybil lists false-positive loudly; continuous scores support graduated rewards and appeals.
4. **Assume composability.** Protocols already bought Passport/Cred; integrators win by **API-after-identity**, not replacement.

## 2. Quadratic funding identity

### Core mechanism

Quadratic funding (QF), popularized by Gitcoin Grants and formalized by Buterin, Hitzig, and Weyl (*Liberal Radicalism*, 2019), matches small donations with a pool such that **breadth of support matters more than depth of wealth**. Optimal QF requires **one person, one unit of influence** — otherwise wealthy actors split across sybil identities to extract the match.

### Identity in QF contexts

Gitcoin’s evolution: **passport scores**, sybil defense rounds, and collaboration with MACI (Minimal Anti-Collusion Infrastructure) for privacy-preserving vote tallying. Lessons:

- **QF without Sybil resistance subsidizes attackers.** Early Gitcoin rounds documented sybil clusters; defense became as important as the matching formula.
- **Pairwise preference matching** (Pairwise Coordination / cluster detection) complements QF by penalizing correlated fake donors.
- **BrightID and Proof of Humanity** were used as identity oracles; friction vs inclusion tradeoff remains acute in Global South participation.

### Collusion resistance

MACI (Chaum-style encrypted voting + zk proofs) prevents bribery/coordination by hiding individual votes while revealing aggregates. Applicability to airdrops: expensive for high-volume social contributions but relevant for **governance-weighted allocation** and retroactive public goods funding.

### Transferable lessons for airdrop allocation

| QF lesson | Airdrop-works implication (Fork A) |
|-----------|--------------------------------------|
| Influence must be person-bounded | Integrate with PoP; don’t rebuild |
| Breadth > depth for “community” | Score thread depth, teaching, not raw likes |
| Sybil defense is iterative | Rubric + farming flag as living defense |
| Transparency builds legitimacy | Explainable judge output for protocol BD |

QF literature supports **Fork A’s positioning**: protocols already understand that **identity alone failed QF rounds**; the next layer is **contribution quality under identity constraints** — exactly the AI Judge wedge.

## 3. LLM-as-judge for content moderation and scoring

### Literature and practice

**LLM-as-judge** emerged from MT-Bench / Chatbot Arena (Zheng et al., 2023) and HELM-style benchmarking: strong LLMs correlate with human preference at lower cost than full human panels. Limitations documented: **position bias, verbosity bias, self-preference**, and instability across prompt phrasing.

**Rubric-based evaluation** (education assessment literature, automated essay scoring) translates to Web3: fixed dimensions with anchors reduce variance. Production pattern: **structured JSON output** with schema validation, not free-form prose scores.

**Content moderation at scale.** Meta’s “LLM-assisted moderation” and OpenAI moderation API show the industry pattern: classifier for clear violations, LLM for edge cases, human for appeals. For **farming detection**, the task is closer to **quality grading + abuse classification** than hate-speech binary classification.

**Gaming and adversarial adaptation.** Goodhart’s Law applies aggressively: optimizers target rubric proxies (thread length, keyword stuffing, AI-generated “educational” slop). Mitigations from literature and practice:

- Multi-signal fusion (LLM + engagement caps + duplicate detection)
- Rotating rubric emphasis (unannounced dimension weights)
- Human audit samples for calibration drift
- **Account-level rollups** (farming percentage) vs single-post scores — harder to game one heroic post

**Cost/latency.** Sonnet-class models at volume: batch async (Celery pattern in airdrop-works architecture) beats synchronous demo streaming for production. Cache by content hash for identical reposts.

**Constitutional AI / policy-as-rubric.** Anthropic’s CAI frames values as principles; product analog: **campaign-specific rubric JSON** per quest/protocol — white-label wedge for Fork A enterprise sales.

### Fork A vs B reuse

The same **judge engine** (prompt → structured scores → flags) serves both forks with different rubric packs:

- **Fork A:** teaching_value, originality, community_impact, farming_flag
- **Fork B:** hook_strength, clarity, brand_fit, predicted_CTR_proxy, fatigue_risk

~70–80% stack reuse (inference pipeline, streaming UX, rubric schema, async scoring); swap rubric, training examples, and GTM surface.

## 4. Pre-launch ad testing literature

### Classical creative pre-testing

Marketing science distinguishes **diagnostic pre-tests** (what’s wrong with an ad) from **predictive pre-tests** (forecast in-market performance). Meta-analysis (e.g., Ipsos, System1, Kantar methodologies) shows:

- Pre-test scores correlate **modestly** with in-market ROI (R² often low for individual ads); value is **rank-ordering creatives** and **eliminating clear losers** before spend.
- **Attention, emotion, and brand linkage** metrics predict recall better than pure “likeability.”
- Small sample concept tests (n≈50–200) suffice for **relative** ranking, not absolute lift prediction.

### Digital / performance marketing norms

Meta Advantage+ and Google Performance Max push advertisers toward **creative volume + automated allocation**. The bottleneck shifted from media buying to **creative iteration speed**. Tools in the Advize / Segwise / Motion class score hooks, pacing, and visual salience — **Fork B’s competitive set**.

### AI creative intelligence

Pattern: ingest ad creative + historical account performance → score variants → recommend winners before budget commit. Moat claims: proprietary outcome data loops. Weakness: **platform API dependence** and **homogenized “AI slop” creatives** that score well on proxies but fatigue in market.

### Pre-launch validation on small budgets

Literature supports **sequential testing**: (1) expert/AI panel rank, (2) micro-spend A/B ($50–500), (3) scale winners. Kill criteria: if AI rank ≠ micro-spend rank consistently, rubric miscalibrated.

### Implications for Fork B

- **Market pull** is real in bull and bear: performance marketers pay when CAC pressure rises.
- **Moat** is crowded; differentiation requires **outcome-linked calibration** or vertical niche (Web3 native ads, creator UGC for protocols).
- **PLG motion** fits: marketer self-serve upload → instant scores → paywall on batch/history.
- **Brand tension:** `airdrop.works` reads Web3/protocol, not Meta advertiser — hurts Fork B unless separate subdomain/brand.

## 5. Platform two-sided markets for reputation

### Rochet-Tirole framework applied

A platform connects two sides and internalizes externalities. For **reputation/trust platforms**:

| Side | Participants | What they want |
|------|--------------|----------------|
| Supply | Contributors, wallets, creators | Fair reward, status, allocation |
| Demand | Protocols, DAOs, brands | Sybil resistance, quality, legibility |

**Who pays?** In Web3, **demand (protocols)** pays — token budgets, grants, BD lines. Supply rarely pays except stake/slash models. This matches **Fork A enterprise sales**, not consumer subscription.

**Cold start.** Reputation platforms face empty marketplace: scores mean nothing without labeled data and adopting protocols. Strategies: (1) single-protocol design partner, (2) public demo (AI Judge landing), (3) integrate as **layer on Passport** rather than standalone identity net.

**Commoditization.** Wallet Sybil APIs trend to **commodity pricing** (Cred ~$0.03/lookup). Vertical SaaS moats: campaign workflow, rubric customization, appeals, on-chain attestation export, creator program ops.

**Network effects.** Weak direct NFX for raw scores (multi-homing across Cred/Trusta/Passport). Stronger NFX if airdrop-works owns **longitudinal contribution history** tied to campaigns — switching cost rises with stored rubric history and farming track record.

**Fork B platform dynamics.** Advertisers (demand) and creative tools/agencies (supply). Stronger PLG, faster cycles, but **more competitive** and less differentiated from generic MarTech unless Web3-vertical.

## 6. Strategic sub-questions — Fork A vs Fork B

### 6.1 Market pull

**Fork A (Reputation / anti-farmer):** Buyers are protocol foundations, token launch teams, creator program leads. Budget sits in **growth/community/grants** lines, often lumpy around TGE and incentive seasons. Bear-market resilient relative to pure ad spend: protocols still need **legitimate distribution narrative** and Sybil cleanup post-mortems (Aptos, LayerZero discourse). Deal size: mid-five to low-six figures annual for integrated judge + reporting; API adjacency lower ACV.

**Fork B (Marketing judge):** Buyers are performance marketers, growth leads, agencies. Budget from **paid social** opex — **procyclical**: cuts first in downturns. Deal size: SMB $50–500/mo PLG; enterprise creative intelligence $20k–100k+. Faster top-of-funnel, lower trust barrier than protocol BD.

**Verdict:** A has **stickier strategic budget** aligned with airdrop.works brand; B has **faster revenue experiments** but higher churn sensitivity.

### 6.2 Competitive moat

**Fork A:** Crowded on **identity** (Passport, Humanity, World). Crowded on **wallet graphs** (Cred, Trusta). **Whitespace:** post-identity **content-quality judge + farming flags + account rollups** for creator programs and retroactive allocation — few combine LLM rubric with Web3-native workflow. Moat = rubric IP + labeled farming data + protocol integrations, not another 0–100 wallet score.

**Fork B:** Crowded (Motion, Segwise, Advize, native platform tools). Moat requires outcome data loop or niche vertical. **Whitespace:** Web3 campaign creative (token launches, quest copy, thread hooks) if rubric trained on that corpus — small niche.

**Verdict:** A offers clearer differentiation from existing codebase narrative; B fights incumbents with bigger budgets.

### 6.3 Technical reuse

**Shared core (~75%):** Anthropic wrapper, rubric JSON schema, async scoring, streaming demo UX, score card UI, caching, rate limits.

**Fork A-specific:** Twitter/crawler ingestion, wallet auth, contribution model, skill tree/XP (optional trim), on-chain export hooks, protocol dashboard.

**Fork B-specific:** Ad creative upload (image/video), hook script fields, brand guideline inputs, Meta/TikTok export formats, performance feedback ingestion for calibration.

**Time-to-MVP:** Fork A **4–8 weeks** to design-partner protocol (demo → API → crawl). Fork B **2–4 weeks** to marketer PLG (new rubric pack + landing split) but **longer** to prove predictive validity without spend data.

**Verdict:** Codebase is **already priced for A**; B is a rubric fork, not a rewrite.

### 6.4 GTM motion

**Fork A:** Enterprise/protocol sales — warm intros, BD cycles 1–3 months, design partner → case study → grants pipeline. Fits **solo founder** if one design partner committed; poor fit for pure PLG at start.

**Fork B:** PLG marketer signup — demo score → email capture → paid tier. Fits **solo** for initial validation; scale requires content marketing and integrations.

**Verdict:** A matches existing `airdrop.works` waitlist and demo; B needs separate acquisition channel and possibly brand.

### 6.5 Brand fit

**Fork A:** `airdrop.works` is **on-brand** — immediate category signal for protocols fighting farmer reputational damage.

**Fork B:** Name **confuses** performance marketers expecting adtech.io positioning; hurts enterprise MarTech credibility unless sub-brand (e.g., `judge.airdrop.works` or external name).

**Verdict:** Strong A fit; B requires brand partition.

### 6.6 90-day shape

**Platform + two wedges (recommended in Episode 1 claim):**

| Track | Wedge | Kill criteria | Success metrics |
|-------|-------|---------------|-----------------|
| **A-primary** | One protocol design partner: ingest X/Twitter contributions → rubric scores + farming % → weekly report | No paid LOI by day 60; partner churns at pilot | 1 paid pilot; ≥500 contributions scored; farming flag precision validated on sample |
| **B-experiment** | Landing split: “Score your ad hook” PLG, Web3-adjacent copy | <100 signups or 0 paid by day 30 | 30-day conversion; rubric vs user satisfaction; optional micro-spend correlation |
| **Core** | Keep AI Judge demo as conversion engine | — | Waitlist growth; demo completion rate |

**Kill B as primary** if PLG fails 30-day gate; **double down A** with stripped gamification and protocol case study.

**Kill A** only if design partner pipeline dead by day 90 *and* B shows paid PLG with repeatable CAC — unlikely without brand pivot.
